# Generating Sales Angles

An angle is not a slogan. It is **a specific person + the moment they feel a
problem + why this product solves it in a way they haven't tried**.

Same product, different angles, different buyers. A neck massager sold as
"desk worker relief" and as "gift for tired parents" are two businesses.

## The avatar comes first

Never write angles for "everyone". For each angle, name:

- **Who** — specific enough to picture them
- **When** — the moment the pain spikes (5pm at the desk, 3am with the baby)
- **What they've tried** — and why it failed them
- **What they'd say** — their words, not marketing words
- **What stops them buying** — the objection this angle must pre-empt

If you cannot fill these in, you do not have an angle yet — you have a product
description.

## The formula

```
Angle = audience segment × pain moment × mechanism × emotional payoff
```

Change any one variable and you get a genuinely different angle worth testing.
Changing only the adjectives gives you the same angle in a new outfit — that is
not a test, it's noise.

## Generating a set

Produce **5–7 distinct angles**, each with:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Name | Internal label, e.g. "desk-worker relief" |
| Audience | The specific person |
| Hook | First line of the ad. Under 10 words. Stops the scroll. |
| Promise | The outcome, concrete |
| Mechanism | Why it works — the believable reason |
| Proof needed | What evidence this angle requires to be credible |
| Main objection | And the one-line answer |

Spread them across different **awareness levels**: some buyers know the product
category, some only know the problem. An angle for each converts different
traffic.

## Copy frameworks

Pick per angle, don't mix inside one piece:

- **PAS** — Problem, Agitate, Solution. Best for a felt, urgent pain.
- **BAB** — Before, After, Bridge. Best when the transformation is visual.
- **AIDA** — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action. Safe default for cold traffic.

## Writing the copy

- Their vocabulary, not yours. Read real reviews and complaints for phrasing.
- Concrete beats clever. "Fits in a carry-on" beats "unparalleled portability."
- One idea per sentence. Cold traffic skims on a phone.
- Specific numbers only when true and sourced.

## Claims that get ad accounts banned

Angles are where these creep in, because they make the pitch stronger. They
also get the account shut down, and a banned ad account usually takes the
business with it.

- **Health or medical claims** — "cures", "treats", "burns fat", "relieves anxiety"
- **Income claims** — "make €5,000/month"
- **Invented statistics** — never fabricate a percentage or a study
- **Fake testimonials** or before/after images that aren't real customers
- **Personal-attribute targeting** — implying you know the reader's health,
  finances, or body. Meta rejects this outright.

If the strongest version of an angle needs a claim you can't substantiate, the
angle is not viable. Find the version that is.

## Testing

Angles are hypotheses. Ship 3–5 at once with equal budget, judge on
cost-per-purchase rather than clicks, and kill on data rather than taste. Then
write the next set of variants around whichever angle won — not around a fresh
guess.
